# 09 - Assembly video script

This is a complete shot-by-shot script for a practical build/explainer video.
It is written for a single presenter with a phone camera and a bench. Nothing
here replaces the written wiring and safety docs.

**Title:** ESP32 Farm Controller - 12 Loads + 5 HP 3-Phase Pump
**Target length:** 18-22 minutes
**Audience:** farm owners, electricians, electronics hobbyists
**Language:** English with optional Tamil captions/voice-over

> No video file is generated by this repository. This is the production script:
> narration, visuals, timings and on-screen text.

## Safety and production notes

- Film the mains section with the isolator off and the pump disconnected.
- Use a licensed electrician on camera for the 415 V termination and testing.
- Never film an open live enclosure. Use a transparent cover or a mock-up for
  the "live" shots.
- Show the RCCB test and E-stop test as separate, clearly labelled shots.
- Keep a fire extinguisher and first-aid kit visible in the workshop.
- Add a persistent lower-third: "230/415 V is lethal. Use a licensed
  electrician."

## Shot list

### 00:00-00:40 - Cold open

**Visual:** Slow pan across the finished panel, then the dashboard on a phone
showing the pump running.

**Narration:**

> "This controller runs twelve farm loads and a five-horsepower three-phase
> borewell pump. It schedules everything, protects the pump against dry run,
> and stays online on a battery during a power cut. It is built from
> off-the-shelf modules, not a custom PCB."

**On-screen text:** `12 loads • 5 HP pump • Wi-Fi • battery-backed control`

### 00:40-01:30 - What we are building

**Visual:** Block diagram `system-block.svg`, slowly zooming.

**Narration:**

> "There are three separate jobs here. The ESP32 decides what should run. The
> relay board switches the small loads. The contactor switches the pump. The
> relay coils run from a mains-derived five-volt supply, so every load drops
> during an outage. The ESP32 and sensors run from a twelve-point-eight-volt
> LiFePO4 battery that charges whenever mains is present, so the controller
> stays alive and can report the outage."

**On-screen text:** `Relay rail = mains-derived` / `Controller rail = battery`

### 01:30-02:30 - Safety first

**Visual:** RCCB, MCB, E-stop, overload, phase-failure relay on the bench.
Close-up of the E-stop and RCCB test button.

**Narration:**

> "Before any wiring: this is lethal mains voltage. The final installation
> must be inspected by a licensed electrician. Use an RCCB, a motor-rated
> breaker, a contactor, a thermal overload, a phase-failure relay and a
> lockable isolator. The E-stop and the float safety contacts are hardwired in
> the contactor coil circuit. The ESP32 is not a safety device."

**On-screen text:** `Licensed electrician required` / `Hardwired safety chain`

### 02:30-03:30 - BOM and tools

**Visual:** Table of parts, then hands sorting them into groups.

**Narration:**

> "The controller is about fifteen thousand six hundred rupees in Chennai
> parts, or about eighteen thousand one hundred with the recommended RCCB and
> phase-failure relay. You need the ESP32 DevKit, a sixteen-channel relay
> module, a five-volt SMPS, a twelve-point-eight-volt six-amp-hour LiFePO4
> battery, a LiFePO4 charger, an LVD, a buck converter, a DS3231, an ACS712,
> four float switches, and the pump starter parts."

**On-screen text:** Link to `hardware/bom.csv`; prices are indicative.

### 03:30-04:30 - Panel layout

**Visual:** `panel-layout.svg`, then the empty enclosure and DIN rail.

**Narration:**

> "Lay out the panel before drilling. Top rail: isolator, RCCB, pump MCB,
> contactor, overload, phase-failure relay, control MCB and the five-volt
> supply. Bottom rail: terminals, relay board, fuse, buck, LVD and more
> terminals. Keep the ESP32 on standoffs with the antenna clear of metal, and
> keep mains and signal wiring in separate ducts."

**On-screen text:** `Top rail = power` / `Bottom rail = control`

### 04:30-06:00 - Mounting the power circuit

**Visual:** Time-lapse of DIN-rail mounting, torque screwdriver, ferrules.

**Narration:**

> "Mount the isolator and RCCB first. Then the pump MCB, contactor, overload,
> phase-failure relay and control MCB. Use ferrules on every stranded wire and
> label both ends. Keep the contactor coil voltage in mind: a 415-volt coil
> needs two phases, a 230-volt coil needs phase and neutral."

**On-screen text:** `Contactor = motor current` / `Relay = coil only`

### 06:00-07:30 - Pump starter and safety chain

**Visual:** `pump-starter.svg`, then close-ups of the actual contactor and
overload.

**Narration:**

> "The three phases go through the isolator, RCCB, MCB, contactor and overload
> to the pump. The control circuit is single-phase, protected by a six-amp MCB.
> The safety chain is a series of normally-closed contacts: E-stop, overload,
> phase failure, tank-full safety float and sump-dry safety float. Any one
> opening drops the contactor. The ESP32 relay is a dry contact in the AUTO
> path. The HAND path uses local start and stop buttons with an auxiliary
> latch. Never bypass the safety chain."

**On-screen text:** `AUTO = ESP32 relay` / `HAND = local start/stop` / `NC chain`

### 07:30-09:00 - Relay module modification

**Visual:** Macro shot of removing the JD-VCC jumper, then wiring VCC/GND/
JD-VCC.

**Narration:**

> "This is the most important modification. Remove the JD-VCC jumper. Power
> the logic side from the ESP32 three-point-three volts. Power the coil side
> from the five-volt SMPS through JD-VCC. Now the ESP32 can stay alive on the
> battery while every relay coil still drops during a mains failure."

**On-screen text:** `REMOVE JD-VCC JUMPER` / `Logic 3.3 V` / `Coils 5 V`

### 09:00-10:30 - ESP32 and relay wiring

**Visual:** `control-wiring.svg`, then point-to-point wiring with labels.

**Narration:**

> "Relay inputs one to twelve go to GPIO thirteen, fourteen, sixteen,
> seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty-one, twenty-two, twenty-three,
> twenty-five, twenty-six and twenty-seven. The pump relay is GPIO thirty-three.
> The inputs are active low, so driving the pin low turns the relay on. Keep
> the relay board's logic ground common with the ESP32, and keep the coil-side
> ground on the SMPS."

**On-screen text:** Pin map table, hold for 10 seconds.

### 10:30-12:00 - Sensors and RTC

**Visual:** Battery divider, mains-sense divider, ACS712 divider, float wiring,
DS3231.

**Narration:**

> "The battery divider is one hundred kilo-ohms and twenty-two kilo-ohms. The
> mains sense watches the five-volt relay rail through twenty-two and fifteen
> kilo-ohms. The ACS712 needs a matched ten-kilo-ohm divider because its output
> can exceed three-point-three volts during pump start. The floats are separate
> safety and sense switches: normally-closed at mains voltage in the contactor
> chain, and normally-open at three-point-three volts for the ESP32. Never mix
> the two commons."

**On-screen text:** `Battery 100k/22k` / `Mains 22k/15k` / `ACS 10k/10k`

### 12:00-13:00 - Battery-backed controller rail

**Visual:** Battery, fuse, charger, LVD, buck, multimeter.

**Narration:**

> "The LiFePO4 battery only backs up the controller. Fuse the positive close
> to the battery. Use a LiFePO4 charger: fourteen-point-four to fourteen-point-six
> volts charge, thirteen-point-five to thirteen-point-six volts float. The LVD
> cuts off around eleven volts. The buck gives a stable five volts to the ESP32.
> Do not parallel the buck output with the relay SMPS."

**On-screen text:** `Controller only` / `LiFePO4 + LiFePO4 charger` / `Pump is not battery-backed`

### 13:00-14:00 - Firmware build

**Visual:** Screen capture of `make compile` and the serial monitor.

**Narration:**

> "The firmware uses the ESP32 Arduino core and ArduinoJson only. Run make
> compile. The web dashboard is embedded into the firmware automatically. The
> build uses about eighty-four percent of the default application partition.
> Upload with make flash, then open the serial monitor at one-fifteen-two
> hundred baud."

**On-screen text:** `make compile` / `make flash PORT=/dev/ttyUSB0`

### 14:00-15:00 - First boot and Wi-Fi

**Visual:** Phone showing `MRAF-xxxxxx`, then the dashboard on the farm Wi-Fi.

**Narration:**

> "On first boot there are no Wi-Fi credentials, so the controller starts an
> access point called MRAF followed by six hex digits. The password is
> farm1234. Connect, open one-nine-two-point-one-six-eight-point-four-point-one,
> log in with admin and farm1234, then scan for your farm Wi-Fi and save. The
> controller reboots and joins the network. Change the default password
> immediately."

**On-screen text:** `AP: MRAF-xxxxxx / farm1234` / `Change the password`

### 15:00-16:30 - Dashboard tour

**Visual:** Screen recording: dashboard, schedules, settings, events.

**Narration:**

> "The dashboard shows mains, battery, tank, sump, pump current and all twelve
> relays. Each relay has Auto, On, Off and Boost. The schedule editor supports
> four windows per day and a Monday-to-Sunday mask. Cross-midnight windows
> belong to the day they start. The pump page has minimum off time, maximum run
> time, dry-run threshold, recovery delay and the resume-after-outage policy."

**On-screen text:** `Auto / On / Off / Boost` / `4 windows per day`

### 16:30-18:00 - Simulator demo

**Visual:** `make sim-scenario SCENARIO=dry-run`, then `outage`.

**Narration:**

> "Before touching the pump, rehearse in the simulator. It uses the same
> dashboard and API, with an accelerated clock. Here is a dry-run fault: the
> pump starts, current stays at zero, and after the grace period the controller
> latches a fault. Here is an outage: all relays drop, the controller stays
> online, and after power returns the pump waits for the recovery delay. The
> same logic runs on the ESP32."

**On-screen text:** `make sim-scenario SCENARIO=dry-run` / `make test`

### 18:00-19:30 - Commissioning tests

**Visual:** Electrician with meter, pump disconnected, then a brief pump run.

**Narration:**

> "With the pump disconnected, test every relay and sensor. Press the RCCB test
> button. Open each safety contact and confirm the contactor drops. Short the
> sense floats and confirm the dashboard changes. Then connect the pump and run
> it briefly in HAND. Check that the current is about seven amps, the pump is
> moving water, and there is no abnormal noise or leak. Finally test an AUTO
> start and stop."

**On-screen text:** `Test with pump disconnected first` / `~7 A running`

### 19:30-20:30 - Maintenance and close

**Visual:** Finished panel, dashboard, farm at dusk with lights on.

**Narration:**

> "Monthly: press the RCCB test button, check for moisture and loose terminals,
> and review the event log. Yearly: check the contactor, torque the mains
> terminals, and test the battery. The controller is a convenience and
> monitoring layer. The safety chain, breaker, RCCB and earth are what keep the
> installation safe. Build carefully, test with the pump disconnected, and use
> a licensed electrician for the mains."

**On-screen text:** `Docs: README.md` / `BOM: hardware/bom.csv` / `Simulator: make sim`
